MEASUREMENT

Measurement, ROI, and ROAS

Day 2 afternoon recap. An anonymized synthesis across four tables. Themes only, no attribution. What you should be doing differently Tuesday morning.

Hosted by Doug Novack. Space provided by William Blair. Cubs game sponsored by ServiceTitan.
Discussions facilitated by Andy Crestodina, Matt Butler, and Mike Rome.
Chatham House Rules: no names, no attribution, patterns only
MEASUREMENT

Attribution is Not Causal ROI

  • The single most repeated frame of the afternoon: what your tools can track is not the same thing as what actually drove the sale.
  • Last-touch attribution persists not because it is right, but because it is the easiest data point to pull by Monday morning.
  • "Attribution turns into a therapy session" was a recurring half-joke and a real diagnosis.
  • The honest question is not "what did my dashboard credit," but "what would top-line revenue have done if I had not done this?"
MEASUREMENT

Three Cautionary Tales: When Operators Turned Marketing Off

  • Three different operators in the room had killed most or all of their paid marketing at some point in their careers.
  • One saw revenue drop roughly 20 to 30 percent within weeks and turned it back on.
  • One saw no change for six months and concluded their spend was misallocated, not absent.
  • One killed only brand-search and traditional TV in different eras; the brand-search shut-off took the Google account months to retrain after the gap.
  • Takeaway: the absence experiment is the most honest measurement experiment most of you have ever run.
MEASUREMENT

The Brand-Search Trap

Brand-search ROAS looks fantastic. It is also the channel you would turn off first to prove incrementality.

  • A consistent provocation from the room: brand-search ROAS looks fantastic, but it is the channel you would turn off first if you had to prove incrementality.
  • People who search your brand by name almost always saw something else first; the ad just intercepts a journey that was already happening.
  • Attribution systems reward end-of-journey events, so platforms keep optimizing budget toward the audience that has already decided.
  • Practical risk: ad automation can quietly stop showing your brand to in-market shoppers because those clicks do not convert within the look-back window.
  • Audit how much of your "demand capture" budget is paying to convert customers you would have closed anyway.
MEASUREMENT

There Is No Such Thing as Direct Traffic

  • Several operators described being shocked by how much of their "direct" or "organic" volume traces back to something else they did upstream.
  • Direct mail drops correlate with same-week spikes in branded search; if you treat them as two separate wins you are paying for the lead twice.
  • The honest read: most "free" traffic is paid traffic with the receipts lost.
  • Implication: when you cut a non-trackable channel and "direct" or "organic" drops, you found your attribution gap.
MEASUREMENT

Demand Capture vs Demand Generation

Most of the room over-invests in capture and under-invests in generation. The math has gotten worse at the bottom of the funnel.

  • A repeated diagnosis: this group, on average, over-invests in demand capture (paid search, LSA) and under-invests in demand generation (video, social statics, awareness).
  • The math has gotten harder in the bottom of the funnel; leads are not getting cheaper and capture-only strategies hit a ceiling.
  • Investing upstream feels worse on the attribution dashboard and better on the business.
  • Tuesday-morning question: what is the smallest demand-gen test you could run in the next 60 days without needing perfect tracking to defend it?
MEASUREMENT

Incrementality Testing is the Modern Bar

  • Several tables converged on incrementality testing as the cleanest way out of the attribution swamp.
  • The point is not to look at attributed conversions, it is to look at total business results in a treated group versus a comparable control.
  • Three viable techniques surfaced: hold-out tests, geo-based tests, and "difference in differences" (paired markets, asymmetric intervention).
  • These work even when attribution is broken, because they read the business directly.
  • "Don’t try to observe what isn’t observable" was the corollary line: model and test the unmeasurable instead of buying a pixel that claims to measure it.
MEASUREMENT

70 Percent Confidence is Enough

  • A useful reframe that landed in the room: most marketing tests demand 95 percent statistical confidence, then never get there, and so nothing ships.
  • One operator’s working rule: act when you are about 70 percent sure. Marketing decisions are reversible; this is not a cancer drug.
  • The risk of inaction is almost always larger than the risk of acting on imperfect data.
  • Pair this with daily monitoring so you can pull the plug fast if a test moves the wrong way.
MEASUREMENT

Send Better Signals Back to the Platforms

  • A recurring tactical theme: stop sending raw form-fills back to Meta and Google as conversions.
  • Several operators are now qualifying harder on the form, or only firing the conversion event when a lead clears a quality bar (booked appointment, qualified disposition).
  • A specific mechanic that surfaced at multiple tables: fork the confirmation page based on how a prospect answers one or two leading-indicator questions, and only fire the conversion event on the qualified path.
  • Meta lead-form leads in particular were called out as low quality; landing pages plus tighter conversion events outperformed in-platform forms in multiple reports.
  • The platforms are only as smart as the signal you feed them. Garbage conversions train them to find more garbage.
MEASUREMENT

LSA: Answer Rate Is the Lever

Operational discipline outranks media buying. The LSA ranking formula rewards picking up, fast, and resolving cleanly.

  • LSA came up at almost every table as a primary acquisition channel and a primary frustration.
  • Operators reported per-call costs in a tight range that scales mostly with answer rate and response time, not bid level.
  • Several tables emphasized: the LSA ranking formula rewards picking up the phone, picking up fast, and resolving the call cleanly. Operational discipline outranks media buying here.
  • LSA visibility into mid-funnel behavior is thin; multiple operators expressed wanting deeper insight than the current platform exposes.
MEASUREMENT

The Customer Journey is the Unit, Not the Lead

  • The unit of measurement that matters is the customer journey, not the lead event or the transaction.
  • New customer vs returning customer separation is the single most under-used segmentation in attribution; many tools blur it by default.
  • Same warning on lifecycle vs acquisition creative: operators routinely show acquisition ads to existing customers and lifecycle ads to people who have never heard of them, without realizing it.
  • Action: audit your suppression lists and your audience targeting before you audit your bids.
MEASUREMENT

Voice of Customer is the Best CRO Input You Are Not Using

  • A pattern surfaced across tables: sales call transcripts, scrubbed of rep language, are a richer source of customer-language insight than any FAQ or persona document.
  • Multiple operators described running won-deal vs lost-deal transcript comparisons through an LLM to surface the questions that get deferred or fumbled.
  • The mechanic: pull transcripts, separate by outcome, strip rep language, ask the model for patterns. Output drives both sales training and high-converting landing pages.
  • Most current site FAQs are guessed by SEO contractors with no access to actual customer calls.
MEASUREMENT

Direct Mail Plus First-Party Data

Mechanical suppression makes direct mail one of the cleanest channels for an incrementality hold-out.

  • The strongest direct-mail conversations in the room were about household-level matching, suppression of current customers, and aligning the mail piece to digital retargeting.
  • Operators described meaningful lift from chaining the mail piece to a personalized landing page (QR or vanity URL) so the offline-to-online transition is observable.
  • The unsolved problem at several tables: clean third-party data for property attributes (pool, roof, equipment age). AI scraping was widely reported as underperforming versus direct sources.
  • Direct mail is also one of the cleanest channels to run an incrementality hold-out on, because suppression is mechanical.
MEASUREMENT

Operating Cadence Beats Tool Stack

  • Many marketing teams cannot see daily business results next to marketing activity in the same view. That gap was a consistent observation from facilitators.
  • The single highest-leverage operating change recommended was a daily view of business outcomes (appointments, sold jobs, revenue) alongside spend and lead volume, by region and channel.
  • Once that view exists, anomalies surface in days rather than quarters.
  • This is an organizational habit, not a software purchase. Most teams already have the data; almost no team has it in one place that gets looked at every morning.
MEASUREMENT

AI in Measurement: What Works, What Backfires

  • The room separated two uses of AI cleanly: analysis and creation.
  • Analysis use cases that landed: call-transcript mining, creative audit across competitors, FAQ generation from voice of customer, generic-copy auditing on web pages, lead scoring and dispositioning.
  • Creative use cases the room was wary of: ad creative that "looks AI" is now being actively penalized in feed by both algorithms and human attention; raw, native, lower-production creative is outperforming polished AI-looking work.
  • A practical implication that emerged: creative diversity (varied formats, hooks, production values) outperforms iterative variations of one winning ad. The motion is to put fresh, varied creative out, not minor tweaks of one winner.
  • The discipline ask from leadership: be able to say clearly where AI is in your stack and where you have decided not to use it, and why.
MEASUREMENT

The Surprises

  • "If I could only turn off one channel, it would be brand search" - provocative, contested, and likely true for more of the room than admitted.
  • Real photos of real employees are now sometimes mistaken for AI; the bar for visible authenticity has moved.
  • Pricing transparency on the site can cost conversion rate but improve lead quality enough to be worth it. Either way, your competitors and AI search are about to publish your pricing for you.
  • The PE growth expectation (mid-single to low-double-digit organic) sits in real tension with the patience that honest incrementality measurement requires. Several operators are living that tension right now.
MEASUREMENT

What to Do Tuesday Morning

  • Put daily business results next to daily marketing data, in one view, and look at it before standup.
  • Audit the conversion events you send back to Google and Meta; tighten them so you stop training platforms on bad signal.
  • Pick one channel and design an incrementality test (hold-out, geo, or paired-market) with a 30 to 60 day window and a 70 percent confidence bar.
  • Pull 50 recent sales call transcripts, separate won from lost, and run them through an LLM for the questions your reps defer.
  • Map your demand-capture vs demand-generation spend ratio honestly. If capture is more than 70 percent of paid, that is the imbalance to address this quarter.
CLOSING / THE MEASUREMENT OPERATING SYSTEM

Daily business data is the cheapest measurement upgrade you can make.

  • Attribution is a tactical tool, not an ROI verdict.
  • Incrementality is the measurement bar; act at 70 percent confidence.
  • Customer journey, not the lead, is the unit.
  • Better signals in, better optimization out.
  • Daily business data is the cheapest measurement upgrade you can make.
