AI Session, Day 2 Morning Recap

Three swim lanes, three rotations, one room of home services marketing leaders. Themes captured across all tables, built for sharing internally with your CEO, IT lead, and direct reports.

Hosted by Doug Novack. Space provided by William Blair. Cubs game sponsored by ServiceTitan.
Discussions facilitated by Andy Crestodina, Justin Massa, and Ryan Prince.
Chatham House Rules: no names, no attribution, patterns only
AI SESSION

Skills Mean Two Different Things

Human capability and AI artifact. Both have to grow together; one without the other stalls.

  • "Skills" first means human capability: the mindsets and habits leaders need to use generative AI well.
  • "Skills" also means a specific AI artifact: a reusable instruction set with a trigger, plain text guidance, and reference files.
  • Treat the AI skill like an employee handbook the model pulls off the shelf when it sees the right cue.
  • Both meanings have to grow together; tooling without human capability stalls, and capability without tooling does not scale.
AI SESSION

Buy the Tool or Build the Skill

  • Vendor SaaS is fine for commodity work, but if everyone can buy the same capability for $20 a month, it stops being a differentiator.
  • Map your capabilities on a spectrum from "cost of doing business" to "core differentiation," then automate the commodity side aggressively.
  • For differentiated work, design human plus AI workflows that extend the moat instead of automating it away.
  • Building your own skills keeps the differentiation inside your walls; buying a tool exports it to a vendor’s roadmap.
AI SESSION

The Proposal and Pipeline Pattern

  • The single highest-impact internal skill pattern observed: take a recorded conversation, draft a proposal or follow-up automatically, leave a short human review at the end.
  • A 12 to 15 page proposal can ship inside an hour of a client call once the skill is tuned.
  • A pipeline push routine that reads the CRM, scans email history, and drafts staged follow-ups in the sender’s voice can reduce a weekly chore to roughly 15 minutes.
  • The pattern translates directly to home services: VoIP transcript in, draft estimate or follow-up out, technician or sales lead in the human loop.
AI SESSION

Don't Fix the Output, Fix the Skill

  • First three runs of a new skill will take longer than doing the work by hand. Resist the urge to hand-edit the output.
  • Edit the skill, re-run, and keep going until the output is consistent. By the fifth pass you have usually worked yourself out of the job.
  • This is a different mindset for executives: you are teaching a system, not finishing a deliverable.
  • The compounding payoff is real but only shows up if you stay disciplined through the awkward early runs.
AI SESSION

Probabilistic vs Deterministic

Knowing which problems need a probabilistic brain and which need rules-based software is a new executive skill.

  • Generative AI is probabilistic: the same input produces a different output each time, like a person.
  • Many marketing and operational workflows still need deterministic behavior (drip campaigns, billing, route logic).
  • The new executive skill is knowing which problems need a probabilistic brain and which need rules-based software.
  • The same generative AI that is probabilistic in conversation is excellent at producing deterministic software, including custom internal tools.
AI SESSION

Context Is the Moat

  • Public LLMs cannot scrape your institutional knowledge: how you define a route, a booking, a service, a customer.
  • The competitive advantage is the private knowledge graph you build around your business, not access to the latest frontier model.
  • Treat your knowledge graph as a private Wikipedia of entities and relationships (customers, contracts, route stops, technicians).
  • Build it once and every AI you adopt gets smarter on your data; skip it and every tool you try will feel generic.
AI SESSION

Standardize Definitions Before You Standardize Tools

  • The room kept returning to a basic problem: every team defines "booking," "sale," "service," "route stop," "customer" differently.
  • Solve this with shared context documents (facts versus insights) where specific teams hold write access and everyone has read access.
  • Lock down the format of high-value fields (customer name, address) so the data is usable by both humans and agents.
  • This is enterprise information architecture work; it is not glamorous but it unlocks everything downstream.
AI SESSION

Pilot Squads Beat Site-Wide Rollouts

Equip a small group with freedom to experiment; deploy managed agents to the rest.

  • Equip a subset of the team (roughly 25 to 30 power users in a few-hundred-person company) with full licenses and freedom to experiment.
  • For everyone else, deploy managed agents into defined workflows so they benefit from outputs without each person inventing their own AI stack.
  • A small cross-functional "tiger squad" with members pulled from connected teams beats a single-department lab.
  • Be loud about wins and failures; the company watches what the CEO praises.
AI SESSION

Middle Managers Are the Adoption Lever

  • The single biggest predictor of frontline AI usage is whether the direct manager holds people accountable for using it.
  • Leadership sets strategy and sponsors the work; middle managers convert it into daily behavior.
  • Stop training people on "where to click" because the buttons move every few weeks; train them on how to think about the tools.
  • The skill being taught now is staying on an exponential improvement curve, not learning any single product.
AI SESSION

Show and Tell Beats Mandates

  • The healthiest adoption cultures run an internal channel where wins, fails, and experiments are visible and the CEO is active.
  • Peer pressure works: gift cards, public recognition, and lightweight competitions accelerate the loop.
  • Most companies already have a "secret" AI side project happening in some department; find it, surface it, fund it.
  • Across the room, executive sponsorship correlated strongly with healthy AI transformation; absence of it correlated with stalled programs.
AI SESSION

Knowledge Trapped in People Is the Real Unlock

Every business has decades of esoteric expertise that lives in a few experienced heads. Capture it.

  • Every business has decades of esoteric expertise (horticulture, HVAC diagnostics, claim adjustment) that lives in a few experienced heads.
  • The new pattern: record the experts talking shop, dump transcripts into the system, let an agent build the knowledge base over time.
  • Internal enterprise search inside the firewall (Google-style search plus a chat layer over your own data) lets a technician on site ask "what does our policy say about this part" and get the right answer in seconds.
  • One operator extracted roughly 2,000 transcripts and 24 GB of email into a 500-note knowledge vault that answers questions about 85% as well as the original expert.
AI SESSION

Persona-Driven CRO Is the Web Unlock

  • Build a synthetic buyer with depth: decision criteria, emotional drivers, objections, who else is in the buying committee, cognitive biases that move them.
  • Feed your AI a detailed persona plus a full-page mobile screenshot of the landing page and ask what is missing.
  • Then layer in the ad they clicked or the keyword that brought them, because the page has to align with the source.
  • Sales call transcripts are the ultimate input; they are literally the voice of your customer.
AI SESSION

Score the Page, Not the Site

  • Top-line website conversion rate is not actionable; it is an average that hides the signal.
  • Look at session key event rate per landing page per traffic source; that is where hypotheses are born.
  • Every URL has a job to do (top-of-funnel content, money page, conversion page); judge each one against its job.
  • The sport of digital is beating yesterday, not chasing a generic industry benchmark.
AI SESSION

Best Practices Are Hypotheses, Not Truths

Stop saying "I think." Say "my hypothesis is" and put a test in front of it.

  • Stop saying "I think" in marketing meetings; say "my hypothesis is" and put a test in front of it.
  • Every change (button color, form length, headline, location of the CTA) should be a testable hypothesis with a defined success metric.
  • AI is excellent at gap analysis: detailed persona plus page screenshot plus ad creative reveals holes humans rarely catch.
  • Multi-site operators were already seeing meaningful conversion lifts (high single digits to low double digits) by tightening the ad-to-page alignment alone.
AI SESSION

Treat Agents Like the Weirdest Junior Employee

  • An agent is just a large language model using tools in a loop to achieve a goal; the buzzword is doing more work than the concept.
  • It is the goofiest junior employee you will ever onboard: super smart in narrow ways, no common sense, no memory, works for pennies, never takes vacation.
  • Decide what it gets read access to and what stays out of reach; one operator gives full read/write across most systems but keeps financial books read-only with a human import step.
  • Probabilistic output means weird phrases will leak in; build a validation step so a second pass catches errors before humans see them.
AI SESSION

Local Beats Cloud for Real Work

  • The room flagged a pattern: cloud chat tools (browser-based ChatGPT and Claude) are great for fast back-and-forth; local tools (Claude Code, Claude’s CoWork, Codex) are where heavy work gets done.
  • Local tools keep files on your machine and only exchange tokens, which lifts the constraints of file size, sandbox limits, and connector restrictions.
  • Local tools can read every transcript from the last four months and produce a synthesis no cloud chat could handle in one shot.
  • IT teams will not love this tomorrow; bring them into the conversation early instead of working around them.
AI SESSION

The Subsidy Ends; Plan Your Token Strategy

We are in the DoorDash-or-Uber phase of generative AI: massively subsidized, deceptively cheap. Build modular.

  • We are in the DoorDash-or-Uber phase of generative AI: massively subsidized, deceptively cheap.
  • Compute shortages and rising token costs are already starting; by late this year and into next year, "Opus for everything" will be financially painful.
  • Build a modular stack so you can move a workload from a frontier model to a cheaper or open-source model without rewriting your skills.
  • Smaller open-source models running locally will be the right answer for many routine tasks; arbitrage your own electricity rather than pay a frontier lab.
AI SESSION

The CEO Has to Be in the Group Chat

  • Across every rotation, the single most predictive pattern for successful AI transformation was visible CEO engagement.
  • A Slack or Teams channel where wins land and the CEO comments outperforms any formal program.
  • Free-version AI usage is not just a waste; it builds false ceilings ("AI cannot do that") that become permanent assumptions and creates security risk because your data trains the model.
  • Pay for the paid plans, give the context, and stop treating AI as a sideline experiment.
AI SESSION

What to Do Monday Morning

  • Pick one repeatable task you do five times a month and would love to stop doing; build the first skill there.
  • Make a copy of your messiest folder and let an AI plan how to organize it (do not work in production).
  • Stand up a show-and-tell channel today; ask your CEO to drop the first comment.
  • Start defining your shared vocabulary (route, booking, service, customer); the knowledge graph is built on those definitions.
  • Audit your top three money pages with an AI-built persona and a full-page mobile screenshot; expect surprises.
AI SESSION / KEY TAKEAWAYS

The moat is your context, your knowledge graph, and your shared definitions.

  • AI skills (the human kind and the artifact kind) compound; vendor purchases plateau.
  • The moat is your context, your knowledge graph, and your shared definitions, not the model you use.
  • Adoption is a culture problem first, a tooling problem second; middle managers and visible CEOs decide the pace.
  • Probabilistic and deterministic systems both belong in your stack; knowing when to use which is a new executive skill.
  • The subsidy ends this year; build modular so you can route work to the cheapest model that can do the job.
