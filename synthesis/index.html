Two Days. One Question.

If you run a home services business and you want sustainable, profitable growth, what should you act on Monday that compounds for three years?

Hosted by Doug Novack and William Blair
Chatham House Rules: no names, no attribution, patterns only
CAPSTONE / SETUP

Two Days. One Question.

  • You came in with quarterly growth pressure and a half-built AI plan, and you sat through three lenses on the same business: investor diligence, AI as an operating model, and measurement that holds up.
  • The question you actually need answered is not "what happened in the room," it is "what do I act on Monday that compounds for three years."
  • This deck is the synthesis, not the recap.
  • One thesis, five reframes, four forks, and a planning posture you can defend to your CEO, your CFO, and your sponsor in the same sentence.
CAPSTONE / THESIS

The Thesis

  • The companies that win the next three years will stop optimizing what is easy to measure and start building what is hard to copy.
  • That single move resolves the attribution argument, the AI strategy argument, and the brand investment argument at once.
  • The hard-to-copy assets are the same three things wearing different names: your brand, your context, and your first-party data.
  • Everything else, including most of your current marketing stack, is rented.
  • The rest of this deck is what changes if you take that thesis seriously.
Five Reframes

How the Job Changes

REFRAME 01

Marketing Is Capital, Not Overhead

You are no longer a cost center justifying budget. You are the steward of growth capital, and that is the seat you should be claiming.

  • Stop benchmarking marketing as a percent of revenue and start treating every dollar like a unit of invested capital with a measurable return.
  • This single reframe changes your conversation with your CFO, your board, and any buyer who walks into your data room two years from now.
  • It also forces the discipline the AI session was pointing at: you stop buying tools and start building capabilities, because capital is patient about payoff and tools are not.
  • The marketing leader is no longer a cost center justifying budget. You are the steward of growth capital, and that is the seat you should be claiming.
REFRAME 02

Attribution and AI Are the Same Conversation

Both pivot on the same executive move: stop optimizing the thing your dashboard makes easy to count.

  • Both pivot on the same executive move: stop optimizing the thing your dashboard makes easy to count, and start building the underlying capability the dashboard cannot see.
  • Attribution measures the last click; incrementality measures whether the business actually moved. AI vendor tools measure the prompt; your private context measures whether the model knows your business.
  • The thing your dashboard credits is almost never the thing that drove the result. The thing your AI tool produces is almost never the thing that differentiates you.
  • If you internalize one collision from these two days, this is it.
REFRAME 03

The Three Compounding Assets

  • Brand, proprietary context, and first-party data are the same asset class wearing three names.
  • Brand is the hedge against the AI search era and the channel buyers ask about before they ask about ROAS.
  • Context (your shared vocabulary, your knowledge graph, your captured expert knowledge) is the moat no public model can scrape.
  • First-party data (customer journey, won versus lost transcripts, household-level history) is what makes every other system smarter.
  • These three compound. Your ad spend, your vendor SaaS, and your tool stack do not.
REFRAME 04

The Demand Stack Has Two Floors

  • You are likely over-invested in demand capture (paid search, LSA) and under-invested in demand generation (brand, video, awareness).
  • This is not an accident. Capture reports cleanly on dashboards; generation does not. So budget flows where the spreadsheet rewards it.
  • The math has gotten worse at the bottom of the funnel. Capture-only strategies hit a ceiling that you cannot bid your way out of.
  • The honest question is not "which campaign performs," it is "what is the smallest demand-generation test I could run in 60 days without needing perfect tracking to defend it."
  • If demand capture is more than 70 percent of your paid spend, you are renting growth that ends the moment you stop paying.
REFRAME 05

Adoption Is a Culture Problem

Tools without culture stall. Culture without tools cannot scale. You need both, in that order.

  • The single biggest predictor of whether AI actually changes your business is not the model, the vendor, or the budget. It is whether your middle managers hold their teams accountable for using it and whether your CEO is visibly in the group chat.
  • The same pattern shows up in measurement. The team that looks at daily business results next to daily marketing data, every morning, before standup, beats the team with the better attribution model.
  • Tools without culture stall. Culture without tools cannot scale. You need both, in that order.
  • This is not an IT project, a marketing project, or an AI project. It is an operating system change.
Four Forks

Decisions Only You Can Make

FORK 01

Brand Search: Cut, Keep, or Quietly Audit

  • The provocation that landed hardest: brand-search ROAS reports best of any channel and is the channel you would cut first if you had to prove incrementality.
  • The room implicitly disagreed on what to do about it. Some operators would cut it tomorrow and watch what direct does. Others would never touch it because the Google account takes months to retrain.
  • This is your fork to make, not ours. The honest middle path: audit how much of your capture budget is paying to convert customers you would have closed anyway.
  • The deeper question this opens: how much of your funnel is genuinely incremental, and how much is a tax you pay platforms to take credit for demand you already created.
FORK 02

Pricing Transparency

  • Publishing pricing on your site usually costs you conversion rate and improves lead quality.
  • Whether that trade is worth it depends on your close rate, your sales operation, and how much wasted time you are absorbing on unqualified leads.
  • The fork that matters: even if you decide against it, AI search is going to publish your pricing for you anyway.
  • You can choose your framing now, or have it chosen for you. Operators who get ahead of this control the narrative; operators who do not show up alongside whatever ranges the model decides to publish.
FORK 03

Mandate or Show-and-Tell

  • The room agreed that visible CEO sponsorship is the single highest correlate with healthy AI transformation. The room did not agree on whether to lead with a mandate or a culture move.
  • Mandate-first: roll out enterprise licenses, set adoption targets, hold managers accountable. Faster, harder, sometimes brittle.
  • Show-and-tell first: stand up a wins channel, fund a tiger squad, let peer pressure pull the rest of the organization along. Slower, stickier, dependent on a CEO willing to be the loudest fan.
  • Most healthy programs end up doing both. The fork is which one you lead with given your specific culture.
FORK 04

Local Equity vs Single Brand

  • The mono-brand mandate is dead. Investors are no longer pushing operators to consolidate everything under one national logo.
  • This is permission, not a directive. The harder question is operating consistency across a family of brands, which is what buyers actually care about.
  • The fork: if you have strong local brand equity, protect it and invest behind it. If you have weak or inconsistent local brands, the case for consolidation still holds.
  • The exception worth flagging: commercial and national-account motions still reward a single brand. If you play in both worlds, your answer might be different in each.
What Now

A Plan You Can Defend

GROWTH MATH

Protect the quarterly number while building the three-year asset.

A daily operating view lets you act fast inside the patience that incrementality requires.

  • The structural pivot in your planning is this: meaningful organic outperformance of your underlying market, sustained over years, is the story buyers reward.
  • That kind of growth does not come from better tactics. It comes from compounding assets (brand, context, first-party data) and from a measurement system that gives you permission to invest aggressively in funnels that are demonstrably working.
  • The PE clock punishes patient measurement. You cannot resolve that tension by ignoring it; you can only resolve it by changing what you measure on the clock.
  • A daily operating view of business outcomes (appointments, sold jobs, revenue) next to spend and lead volume, by region and channel, lets you act fast inside the patience that incrementality requires.
  • This is how you protect the quarterly number while building the three-year asset.
NEXT 90 DAYS

The Next 90 Days

  • Build the daily operating view. Business outcomes next to spend and lead volume, in one place, looked at before standup.
  • Audit the conversion events you send back to Google and Meta. Stop firing on raw form-fills; fire on qualified leads or booked appointments.
  • Run one incrementality test (hold-out, geo, or paired-market) on one channel with a defensible confidence threshold and enough time to see real signal.
  • Pull a stack of recent sales call transcripts, separate won from lost, run them through an LLM, and rewrite your top money pages from what you find.
  • Stand up a show-and-tell channel and ask your CEO to drop the first comment this week.
NEXT 12 MONTHS

The Next 12 Months

  • Define your shared vocabulary (route, booking, service, customer) and lock down the format of your highest-value fields. This is the foundation everything downstream sits on.
  • Build the private knowledge graph. Capture the experts talking shop, ingest the transcripts, let the system get smarter than the next hire.
  • Shift the demand-capture to demand-generation ratio toward something more balanced. Most of the room is over-rotated toward capture; you will need to deliberately fund the generation side.
  • Close the attribution gap to a workable confidence threshold and use that confidence to push more capital through the funnels that work.
  • Get the marketing and sales scorecard onto a single page and present it that way to your board.
CAPSTONE / THE POSTURE

Stop optimizing what is easy to measure. Start building what is hard to copy.

  • Manage marketing as capital, not as overhead.
  • Treat AI and measurement as the same operating-system upgrade.
  • The companies that hold this posture for three years will outperform the ones chasing the next tactic by every metric that matters at exit.

Brand, context, and first-party data are the only three things that compound. Everything else is rented.
